NEO Gamma costs $20,000 to own on the early-access plan, or $499 a month if you subscribe instead. Both numbers are on the 1X order page, and both are confirmed on the catalog row. A $200 deposit is refundable and holds a place in line. It is not the full price, and it is not proof that a robot has left the factory for your house.

HomeBotRadar keeps the catalog name NEO Gamma. 1X now brands the consumer product as NEO and describes it as the successor to earlier Gamma and Beta hardware. The price below is for that consumer pre-order, not for a separate Gamma leftover.

## The $20,000 pre-order

The $20,000 path is ownership. The order page includes a 3-year warranty and puts those buyers ahead for 2026 US delivery. Tax and shipping are the usual extras a checkout adds. The catalog price field is $20,000 because that is the ownership number, not the monthly plan. If you compare robots by the price column alone, you are looking at the buy path.

The hardware sheet on the same order page lists about 168 cm, about 30 kg, and about 4 hours of runtime on an 842 Wh pack. Hands are listed at 22 degrees of freedom each. Those specs are confirmed from that sheet when the robot page matches it. They do not mean every chore in a demo video is autonomous on day one. 1X describes Expert Mode, a scheduled remote operator, for tasks the robot has not learned yet.

## $499 a month

The subscription is $499 a month and is scheduled for later delivery than the ownership plan. Read it as a lease of the early-access robot, not as a cheaper way to get the same 2026 priority. The $200 deposit still applies. Over a few years the monthly plan costs more than $20,000, which is the point of checking both numbers before you pay the deposit.

There is no public price for a second country. The catalog countries list is the United States. A shopper outside the US should treat the page as a US offer until 1X says otherwise.

## A 2026 window, not a doorstep date

The order page still says US deliveries start in 2026. The robot page was rechecked on November 3, 2026, and that sentence was still there. An April 30 factory note said units coming off the line were prioritized for internal home testing. The order page has not announced that a customer shipment has started. "Starts in 2026" can still mean late in the year, and it can still slip. Do not read the deposit as a delivery appointment.
