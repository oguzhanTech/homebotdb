A staircase is a different problem from a flat hallway. The catalog robot that publishes a step height is Spot. On the Boston Dynamics product page, the locomotion table lists a max step height of 300 mm (11.8 in) and a max slope of ±30 degrees. Walking height sits around 610 mm, and the robot weighs about 33.8 kg with a battery. That is a stair-sized step for a typical indoor flight. It is also an inspection platform, not a chore robot for a family.

## Spot is not a home checkout

Boston Dynamics sells Spot to factories, energy sites, labs, construction, and public safety. The page talks about autonomous charging, inspection packages, and a payload of about 20 kg. It does not offer a living-room price. HomeBotRadar keeps the last public Explorer-kit figure, about $74,500 in 2020 for the robot, batteries, charger, and tablet. The price status is an estimate. A configured robot with an arm and software costs more, and the company quotes qualified buyers. Commercial, industrial, and academic customers are the stated market. A homeowner who wants Spot for the stairs is asking for an enterprise sale.

## The humanoids you can order

Unitree G1 is Buy Now at $13,500. The official spec page lists a standing height of about 132 cm, a weight of about 35 kg, a knee torque figure, and a depth camera plus 3D LiDAR. It does not list a step height, a slope limit, or a stair mode. A missing line is not a promise that the robot will take your staircase, and it is not a lab test either. Treat the G1 as a compact humanoid with a cart, often backordered, until Unitree prints a stair number.

NEO Gamma is the US home pre-order, at $20,000 or $499 a month. The order page is about early access, a deposit, and 2026 US delivery. It does not rate the robot for stairs. A home humanoid that can walk on a show floor still has to be rated for your steps, your landings, and what happens if it falls. That rating is not on the page.

## Apartments and the list that leaves humanoids out

If the home has stairs, do not read a two-leg body as a yes.
