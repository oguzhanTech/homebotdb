A Tokyo company is treating a broken workplace humanoid like a stalled car. **GMO AIR** (GMO AI & Robotics, part of GMO Internet Group) said on **Tuesday, September 8, 2026** that it would start running a dedicated service van it calls the **GMO Humanoid Ambulance**. [GMO's note](https://ai-robotics.gmo/news/article/gmo-humanoid-ambulance/) says the van carries diagnostic gear, spare parts, tools, and a replacement humanoid. Engineers ride along. If they cannot finish the job on the floor, they swap the spare and haul the broken body back to **GMO Humanoid Lab** in Shibuya.

This is not a robot that repairs other robots. It is not self-driving. A person drives. A person opens the panels.

## Not a public emergency number

GMO says the van is Japan's first vehicle designed around humanoid repair. That is the company's own web check as of September 2026, not a government certificate. Dispatch is not automatic. The van is meant for machines **GMO AIR already put into a site**, and GMO will decide case by case.

There is one vehicle for now, aimed at central Tokyo, with more bases later if demand shows up. GMO does not manufacture a humanoid. The catalog robot that matches the hardware they race and sell around is **Unitree G1**.

[ITmedia](https://www.itmedia.co.jp/news/article/2609/08/2000001266/) toured the cabin: a stretcher sized for a humanoid, plus parts and laptops. The white-and-red look is meant to read as an emergency van. Designer Yasumichi Morita (GLAMOROUS) handled the styling.

If a repair will take time, customers on GMO's existing **¥70,000 a month** plan (about **$470**) can take a spare on the spot. That plan also includes up to **1.5 extra months** of a rental body a year at no extra fee. Treat those yen figures as a commercial lease, not a home shop price.

## A cracked head in Beijing

One spark was the World Humanoid Robot Games in Beijing in August. GMO ran a **custom Unitree G1** in the 100, 400, and 1,500 meter races. In a 400-meter heat the robot fell and cracked its head. Engineers had about **20 minutes** before the next round. They had it walking again in about **10 minutes** with spare parts they already had on site.

Senior research engineer Shota Takizawa said that after about **three months** of use, **three or four in ten** machines have some part fail, depending on the job. Hard motion tests have broken almost every humanoid they tried. CEO Tomohiro Uchida said tougher workplaces will mean more breakage, and more need for people who can show up.

A **Unitree G1** you can order today still needs a human with parts when a joint or a head cover gives out. A hotel or warehouse that leans on two-legged staff cannot wait for a crate to go back to the lab. Homes are not in this launch. The van is field service for commercial floors, not a product you add to Compare.

We are not adding a GMO ambulance row to the catalog. There is no consumer cart for the van. **Unitree G1** is already listed if you want the body GMO raced.
