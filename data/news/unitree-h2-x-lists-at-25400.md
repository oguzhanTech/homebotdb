Unitree lists the H2-X at $25,400 before tax and shipping. The clip of it lifting a car is a promo. On the same product page, the arm is rated at about 15 kg at peak and about 7 kg for steady work.

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Y3zb4o47yqA

The [H2-X page](https://www.unitree.com/H2-X/) sells a full-size humanoid for security, factory, and commercial jobs. Standing size is 1,725 mm tall, about 173 cm, and 65 kg or more with the battery. There are 31 joints: six in each leg, seven in each arm, three at the waist, and two in the head. A manual controller is in the box. The education version, H2-X EDU, is quote-only. That is the one Unitree marks for secondary development. The standard H2-X is not.

## Eight hours, on Unitree's sheet

The page lists about 8 hours standing still and about 8 hours of continuous walking. Power is two batteries you can swap while the robot is in use: 20 amp-hours, about 1.15 kilowatt-hours. Unitree says the numbers can change with the setup. The sheet does not say the robot can fight, or lift, for eight hours.

## What the video shows

On October 21, 2026, Unitree posted a short film called H2-X Kung Fu Legend Guardian. The robot jumps onto cars, throws kicks, and plays a guard in staged scenes, including raising the front of a car so someone can be pulled out. Unitree does not say whether a person was driving those moves. The product page is plainer than the film: this is a civilian robot, keep a safe distance, and do not modify it into something dangerous. Some features are still in development. The page also tells individual buyers to learn the limits of humanoid robots before they pay.

H2-X is not joining the HomeBotRadar catalog. It is a security and commercial body with a controller in the box, not a home helper we score for a living room. The Unitree humanoid we track for shoppers is Unitree G1, listed from $13,500.
