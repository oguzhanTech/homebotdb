A **73-year-old** Arizona State University parking attendant is taking **Starship Technologies** to court over a campus food-delivery robot collision. [Yahoo News NZ](https://nz.news.yahoo.com/parking-attendant-73-sues-robot-192316284.html) republished [The Independent](https://www.independent.co.uk/news/world/americas/parking-attendant-sues-delivery-robot-b3021163.html) on the suit, citing NBC affiliate **12 News**. We are filing it for HomeBotRadar readers on **July 25, 2026**.

**Trudy Perez** says one of Starship's wheeled delivery bots knocked her down in a Tempe ASU parking structure in late **2023**, leaving a **spinal fracture** and lasting impairment. Headline copy sometimes calls the machine a "drone." Court and police accounts describe a ground robot, the same six-wheeled campus delivery bots Starship runs on many universities.

## What police and the complaint describe

According to ASU Police Department reporting summarized by [KYMA](https://kyma.com/news/arizona-news/2026/07/24/arizona-woman-sues-food-delivery-robot-company-for-injuries/) and [KJZZ](https://www.kjzz.org/business/2026-07-14/lawsuit-claims-asu-employee-was-struck-injured-by-a-food-delivery-robot):

- Perez was working in **Parking Structure 1** when a white Starship robot crossed in front of her
- She waited, then walked behind it
- The robot **reversed** into her and knocked her onto her back
- It moved away, then **reversed again** toward her while she was still on the ground
- An officer noted an about **4-inch** cut near her left elbow, back pain, and trouble walking
- Starship later gave police **video from the robot** and asked for her contact details for insurance and "promo codes"

Vrana Law Firm, representing Perez, said she suffered a spinal fracture and other injuries after the robot "suddenly changed direction" and reversed into her. The suit alleges Starship failed to operate the device safely, that the robot was still being tested and defective, and that a remote monitor failed to exercise control.

## How Starship is answering

Starship has declined public comment on the active case in press coverage. Court filings reviewed by KJZZ say the company argues Perez's injuries stem from her own negligence, preexisting conditions, and a third party. A jury trial is calendared for **early 2027** (February in several local reports).

Arizona law requires operators of autonomous delivery devices to carry at least **$100,000** liability coverage per unit, keep reasonable pedestrian precautions, and have a **human monitor** watching each robot. Local attorney **Josh Kolsrud**, not involved in the case, told 12 News those rules exist to protect both pedestrians and operators.
